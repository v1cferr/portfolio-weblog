I've used Linux since around 2022. I started on Linux Mint, moved to Ubuntu,
reached Arch in early 2024 and, in July 2026, NixOS. This time it's for good: my
whole infrastructure is in Nix now, and I like it that way.

This post is about that last move. Almost everything here has a commit behind it
in the [v1cferr/dotfiles](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles) repository.

## Why I left an Arch that worked

Arch worked. The problem was something else: I was already trying to make it
**declarative**. Since August 2025 I had kept a dotfiles repository with
Hyprland, Waybar and zsh, managed with `stow`
([first commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/7e747854)). The idea
was to describe my system in files and recreate it whenever I needed.

But it kept getting worse. There was a lot of _config drift_: what was in the
repository and what was actually on the machine drifted apart over time. It was
awful to maintain. And there was no easy way to install exactly my system on
another machine.

When I went looking for a way out, I found NixOS, and the way it works,
genuinely declarative, helps a lot. The decision was mine: I had been studying
NixOS for a while. In July 2026 I wrote the migration plan
([`NIXOS-MIGRATION.md`](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/blob/nix-flake-skeleton/NIXOS-MIGRATION.md),
in Portuguese), put together with Claude Code's help. That day's Arch inventory
showed how big the thing was:

- 222 days on that install;
- 1,566 packages, 35 of them from the AUR;
- Hyprland with Quickshell, and 16 Docker containers serving my `*.v1cferr.dev`.

The plan's diagnosis summed up what I felt: the setup was already "a manual,
imperative reimplementation of NixOS".

## Safety net first

Before touching anything, I kept the past. The nearly 500 commits of the Arch
era live in tags in the same repository, such as `archive/arch-final`.

That same week I found out one of my NVMe drives, a Netac, was dying. I rescued
its data, 27 GB, with zero loss
([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/5cb4f7a6)).

## From scratch, for real

I wanted to start from zero on a new distro. So I began with the minimal NixOS
ISO and a literally empty repository: on July 16, 2026 I reset the history
([`chore!: reset history`](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/79f5d4a5))
and built it up piece by piece, until it was exactly the way I wanted.

The first step was a single flake holding the system and Home Manager together
([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/60950c75)): one `rebuild`
applies both.

## Three disks, one flake

The migration went through three disks before reaching its final home:

1. A Seagate HDD, where the first version was born.
2. A SanDisk SSD, with its partitioning already declared in
   [disko](https://github.com/nix-community/disko)
   ([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/45455b58)). On July 22,
   the cutover script left the repository with the message "migração
   Arch→SanDisk concluída" (Arch→SanDisk migration done)
   ([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/be6d2f04)).
3. The Kingston NVMe that used to hold Arch, formatted with btrfs on August 1
   ([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/1438a965)).

With the partitioning declared in disko, every disk swap is written down in the
repository itself. The data came over through restic backups.

## The desktop back, piece by piece

I went quickly through GNOME and XFCE before landing on Hyprland
([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/33e21dfc)), now configured
in Lua ([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/7d55bb01)), with the
Quickshell bar brought over from Arch
([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/174108b5)).

A few problems that turned into lessons:

- **Monitors on Wayland:** the TV's connector was `HDMI-A-1`, not `HDMI-1`
  ([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/ab12501d)).
- **Dark Qt theme:** Dolphin stayed light until I pinned `QT_QPA_PLATFORMTHEME`
  in Hyprland's environment
  ([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/f6e96342)).
- **VS Code keyring:** under Hyprland it doesn't detect `gnome-libsecret` on its
  own ([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/933bd2a7)).

## Secrets done right

Passwords and keys live in [sops-nix](https://github.com/Mic92/sops-nix),
encrypted with age
([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/27ecea31)). The source of
truth is Bitwarden, and a `sync-secrets` command brings them into the repository
([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/c5ff005a)). Later I added a
second age key, so there is no single point of failure
([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/f738005f)).

## The GPU swap

On July 24 I swapped the RTX 3050 for an Intel Arc B580
([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/4ba13014)). It had to do
with Linux in general: NVIDIA's proprietary driver was always the sore point,
and the Arc runs on an open driver. The 3050 was also too weak for the QHD
monitor I use now, a 180 Hz ASUS ROG Strix XG27ACS (the rest is in
[my setup](/en-us/setup)).

The Arc brought two lessons: a hang on reboot, fixed with `reboot=pci`
([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/59c26ce2)), and the rule
that Intel's media driver can't come from a newer channel than libva, or video
silently falls back to the CPU.

## Measuring "done"

To know when the migration was over, I ran a parity audit across 53 Arch
configuration areas
([arch-parity-audit.md](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/blob/main/docs/arch-parity-audit.md)).
On August 5 I closed the Arch chapter: the old home is kept in a 44.6 GiB restic
backup, checked with no errors
([commit](https://github.com/v1cferr/dotfiles/commit/dd7af239)). On October 13 I
declared the migration complete.

## What the repository became

Today the same flake describes more than my main machine: a ThinkPad T480s
laptop, the router and even my phone. Putting exactly my system on the T480s,
with the same window manager, icons and themes, was very easy, and that is what
I never managed on Arch.

Every commit goes through `nix flake check` in CI, decisions become ADRs, and
everything is documented at
[dotfiles.v1cferr.dev](https://dotfiles.v1cferr.dev).

## What I miss, and what comes next

Honestly? Maybe how easy the AUR was, always with the newest packages. On NixOS,
if a package isn't in the `unstable` channel, packaging it, writing its flake
and keeping it versioned takes work.

But that became part of the path too. On Arch I already maintained AUR packages
([perssua](https://aur.archlinux.org/packages/perssua) and
[spider-git](https://aur.archlinux.org/packages/spider-git)); on NixOS I
maintain my own packages and flakes, about 22 of them in `pkgs/` today. The next
step is to publish something for the community.

And the system won't stop evolving, because neither will I.
