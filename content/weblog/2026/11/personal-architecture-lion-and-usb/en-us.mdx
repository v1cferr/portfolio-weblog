_Translation:_ The lion reminds me of everything I have been through. The USB

I have two tattoos. The Alliance lion, from World of Warcraft, is on the inner
side of my left forearm, from around late 2023. The USB trident is on my right
wrist, from around early 2024. The dates are approximate until I check the
photos from back then.

The [tattoos page](/tattoos) shows both, with photos and what each one means to
me. This post is the rest of the story: what lies behind the USB symbol, where
it came from, and why, years later, I still think I made the right choice.

## Two marks, two moments

First came a symbol of courage and endurance. Then a symbol of the relationship
I wanted to keep with technology for the rest of my life. I did not plan them as
a system; that reading came later, looking back.

The lion is my war mark:

> Essa tatuagem representa todas as minhas lutas desde que fiz ela no final
> de 2023.

_Translation:_ This tattoo stands for all my struggles since I got it, at the
end of 2023.

The USB is my anchor:

> não me arrependo nenhum pouco das duas tatuagens, estou vendo um video sobre
> wow agora e só de ouvir a soundtrack já fico lacrimejando sério e sem falar no
> USB no meu pulso é minha ancora, pois sempre gostei de tecnologia e vou morrer
> envolto dela.

_Translation:_ I don't regret either of the two tattoos one bit. I'm watching a
video about WoW right now and just hearing the soundtrack makes my eyes water,
seriously, not to mention the USB on my wrist, which is my anchor, because I
have always liked technology and I'll die surrounded by it.

When I got them, I believed I would not regret them. I am still happy with them:

> sinceramente, ainda bem que fiz essas duas tatuagens. eu lembro que o eu de
> 2-3 anos atrás pensava que eu realmente não iria me arrepender e ele estava
> certo.

_Translation:_ Honestly, I'm glad I got these two tattoos. I remember the me of
two or three years ago thought I really wouldn't regret them, and he was right.

## Dissecting the trident

The tattoo is not a USB-A or USB-C connector. It is the standard's glyph: a
round base, a central shaft and three branches ending in different shapes, an
arrow, a square and a circle.

What can be said about it: the trademarked trident logo was created by the
USB-IF while the 1.0 specification was being written, and the different shapes
at the tips stand for the many kinds of device one port can serve
([IEEE](<https://ethw.org/Milestones:Universal_Serial_Bus_(USB),_1996>)). There
is also a popular story that the drawing imitates Neptune's trident
([Gizmodo, 2010](https://gizmodo.com/the-secret-histories-of-those-ing-computer-symbols-5612630)),
but I found it in no official source, and nobody knows for sure who designed the
symbol
([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/2239883/what-usb-trident-symbol-means/)).

On top of that I have my own reading, which is mine and not the USB-IF's:

| Part of the drawing | What I read in it                              |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Round base          | The origin: my story, before the tools         |
| Central shaft       | Continuity, a core of values                   |
| Branches            | The paths, fields, projects and relationships  |
| Arrow               | Direction, curiosity, moving toward the future |
| Square              | Logic, structure, code and engineering         |
| Circle              | Human connection, creativity and exchange      |

What interests me most in the drawing is the idea of joining different things
through a common structure. I also like that I did not pick the logo of a
language, an operating system or a company, but the symbol of a standard shared
by a whole ecosystem.

## Where USB came from

In the 1990s, plugging a peripheral into a PC could mean serial and parallel
ports, separate interfaces for keyboard, mouse and joystick, and expansion cards
with SCSI or proprietary protocols; sometimes you had to install hardware just
to get the right port
([IEEE](<https://ethw.org/Milestones:Universal_Serial_Bus_(USB),_1996>),
[IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/how-usb-came-to-be)).

USB began as an idea from Ajay Bhatt, an Intel engineer who pushed inside the
company to replace those ports with a single interface and wrote the first draft
of the specification. But he did not do it alone: from 1994, seven companies
(Compaq, DEC, IBM, Intel, Microsoft, NEC and Nortel) developed the standard
together, the USB-IF was formed in 1995 and the USB 1.0 specification came out
on January 15, 1996
([IEEE](<https://ethw.org/Milestones:Universal_Serial_Bus_(USB),_1996>),
[Intel, 1998](https://www.intel.com/pressroom/archive/releases/1998/US062598.HTM)).
That collective origin suits what USB is: a standard that works only because
many people adopt it.

When I learned that story, I wrote:

> Isso aqui me deixou mais feliz de ter essa tattoo. Não vou mentir

_Translation:_ This made me happier to have this tattoo. I won't lie.

## Four wires and a standard

The original USB used a four-conductor cable: `VBUS` (5 V), `GND` and the
`D+`/`D−` pair. The two data wires are not "one to send and one to receive":
they form a single differential pair that carries data both ways
([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/how-usb-came-to-be),
[USB in a NutShell](https://www.beyondlogic.org/usbnutshell/usb2.shtml)).

It also let you plug and unplug devices with the computer on (_hot plug_), and
it adopted Plug and Play's self-identification. Neither idea was born with USB:
Plug and Play, FireWire and ACCESS.bus were already heading that way, but USB is
what took them to every PC
([IEEE](<https://ethw.org/Milestones:Universal_Serial_Bus_(USB),_1996>)).

Under the gesture of plugging something in there is a whole sequence.
Simplified:

```text
Physical connection of the device
        ↓
Detection by the host
        ↓
Enumeration: descriptors, address
        ↓
Driver picked by vendor/product ID
        ↓
Transfers through endpoints
        ↓
Kernel and applications
```

([USB in a NutShell, ch. 5](https://www.beyondlogic.org/usbnutshell/usb5.shtml)
and [ch. 6](https://www.beyondlogic.org/usbnutshell/usb6.shtml))

The standard evolved without losing its identity:

| Generation        | Reference capability    |
| ----------------- | ----------------------- |
| USB 1.x (1996)    | 1.5 and 12 Mbit/s       |
| USB 2.0 (2000)    | 480 Mbit/s              |
| USB 3.x and USB4  | 5, 10, 20 and 40 Gbit/s |
| USB4 Version 2.0  | Up to 80 Gbit/s (2022)  |
| USB PD 3.1 (2021) | Up to 240 W of power    |

These are nominal figures, not promises for any cable or port: the real speed
and power depend on what both sides negotiate
([USB-IF, speeds](https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/usb_data_performance_language_usage_guidelines_jan_2024.pdf),
[USB4 v2.0](https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/USB-IF%20USB%2080Gbps%20Announcement_FINAL.pdf),
[USB PD 3.1](https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/2021-05/USB%20PG%20USB%20PD%203.1%20DevUpdate%20Announcement_FINAL.pdf)).

That is an important metaphor for me: keeping an identity does not require
keeping the same implementation forever.

## Royalty-free, but not free

People often say Intel "gave up the royalties" on USB. The verifiable part is
more precise: companies that sign the USB adopters agreement join a reciprocal,
royalty-free patent licence for compliant products
([USB-IF](https://usb.org/node/1723)), but using the logo requires a licence and
compliance testing ([USB-IF](https://usb.org/node/1811)), and a _vendor ID_
costs money ([USB-IF](https://www.usb.org/getting-vendor-id)). Bhatt himself
never earned money from USB, because Intel owns the patent
([NPR](https://www.kpbs.org/news/2019/06/21/ever-plugged-a-usb-in-wrong-of-course-you-have)).

The point that stays is simple: a universal interface depends on cooperation,
and its value grows with the number of systems that can talk to each other.

## The language of machines

Technology draws me for two reasons that go together: it makes my life better,
and I like understanding how it does that.

> Na época que decidi fazer ela, eu já sabia que nunca ia parar de gostar de
> tecnologia. Principalmente pq facilita minha vida e consigo usar ao meu favor.
> Mas também gosto de entender a linguagem das máquinas e como tudo isso
> funciona under the hood.

_Translation:_ When I decided to get it, I already knew I would never stop
liking technology. Mainly because it makes my life easier and I can use it in my
favour. But I also like understanding the language of machines and how all of it
works under the hood.

The same holds beyond USB: between an application and the hardware there are
APIs, libraries, system calls, the kernel and drivers; between an HTTP request
and the wire there are sockets, packets and frames; between a chat with an AI
and the GPU there are tokens, a model and inference. I do not need to master
every layer. I can use an abstraction when it is enough and go down when I have
a reason. The better I understand a system, the better I can diagnose it,
automate it and decide whether I want to depend on it. That is where knowledge
turns into autonomy.

> The interface changes. The need to understand stays.

Today I work with systems and artificial intelligence, and the tattoo predates
that. The work only added a concrete layer to a choice that already existed:

> esse simbolo USB representa muito pra mim, ainda mais que uso a tecnologia
> para facilitar minha vida e meu trabalho, vivo tecnologia todos os dias

_Translation:_ This USB symbol means a lot to me, especially since I use
technology to make my life and my work easier; I live technology every day.

And if USB ever leaves the stage, the symbol still holds, the way the floppy
disk is still the save icon:

> USB não vai morrer tão cedo e mesmo que fique ultrapassado e inutilizado,
> ainda vai ser parte da minha historia já que uso praticamente pra tudo. por
> isso decidi fazer na epoca tbm e tenho orgulho até hoje

_Translation:_ USB won't die any time soon, and even if it becomes outdated and
unused, it will still be part of my story, since I use it for practically
everything. That's also why I decided to get it back then, and I'm still proud
of it.

## Personal architecture

The phrase came up while I was organizing these ideas, and I liked it:

> arquitetura pessoal talvez realmente seja a palavra

_Translation:_ personal architecture may really be the word

It is a metaphor from systems architecture: fairly stable principles, structures
that hold up everyday life, and room to learn and change. The lion is endurance
and memory; the USB is adaptation and connection. Strength without adaptation
turns into rigidity; adaptation without anchors makes it hard to carry on. The
two tattoos help me think about that balance, without forgetting that people are
not applications: they do not need constant performance, nor to turn rest into
productivity.

The metaphor I like most is backward compatibility: staying myself while I
update the implementation. I can value the kid who liked computers while I learn
what he did not know yet.

A third tattoo, the golden ratio, is only an idea: I have not got it and do not
know if I will. It is on the [tattoos page](/tattoos#golden-ratio) as an idea,
not as something I have.

## A message to my future self

If I read this many years from now, I want to remember who I was when I chose
these two symbols. At the end of 2023 I put the lion on my arm; at the start of
2024, the USB on my wrist. I did not know what shape my life would take, but I
recognized two things I wanted to carry: courage to face whatever came, and
curiosity to keep discovering the world.

The music of World of Warcraft still moves me. I am still fascinated when I find
out how a machine works. Maybe one day the computers, languages and models I use
today will be museum pieces, and maybe even USB will disappear. None of that
erases what I lived through them.

If the future turns out different from what I imagine, I do not want these
symbols to become a demand to live exactly as I planned. I want them to remind
me that I can change, start over and keep the parts of me that still matter.

> acho foda demais e tenho orgulho de ter essas duas.

_Translation:_ I think they're freaking awesome, and I'm proud to have these
two.
